# Universidad Nacional Autónoma de México (UNAM) – Brand Identity Guidelines

## 1. Introduction

This document outlines the core visual identity elements of the Universidad Nacional Autónoma de México (UNAM). Its purpose is to ensure consistent, coherent, and recognisable use of the university’s logos, colours, typography, and other brand assets across all media and applications.

UNAM’s brand image is built on a set of cultural symbols – from its solemn coat of arms and anthem to its dynamic athletic mascot. A unified visual identity reinforces the institution’s prestige, fosters cohesion among its diverse faculties and departments, and projects professionalism and academic rigour to the public.

---

## 2. Core Identifiers

### 2.1 Coat of Arms (Escudo)

UNAM’s coat of arms was designed in 1921 by then‑rector José Vasconcelos. Its central motif is a two‑headed bird – the Mexican eagle and the Andean condor – jointly guarding a map of Latin America. This design embodies Vasconcelos’ philosophy of *la raza cósmica* (the cosmic race), symbolising the fusion of the Old and New Worlds and the unity of Latin America.

The coat of arms is UNAM’s most important symbol, carrying profound historical and cultural meaning.

### 2.2 Motto (Lema)

The university’s motto is:

> **“Por mi raza hablará el espíritu”**  
> (“Through my race, the spirit shall speak”)

This motto expresses UNAM’s mission and ideals, reflecting its commitment to Mexican national identity and cultural heritage.

### 2.3 Athletic Identity – The Puma

On 20 April 1974, UNAM officially adopted the puma logo, designed by Manuel Andrade “El Pajarito”. The original mark features a stylised puma head encircling the letter “U”. The modern version places the puma inside a rounded triangle, representing UNAM’s three core pillars: **teaching** (*docencia*), **research** (*investigación*), and **cultural outreach** (*difusión de la cultura*).

## 4. Typography

Recommended typefaces for UNAM and its dependencies include:

- **Gotham** – for headlines and large display text  
- **Booster Next FY** – for body copy  
- **Myriad Pro** (Black weight) – used in the DGTIC logo and specific applications  
- **Arial / Helvetica** – recommended for general use in some faculties

Each faculty and dependency may choose appropriate typeface combinations within this framework, provided they adhere to the overall brand guidelines.

---

## 5. Logo Usage Guidelines

### 5.1 Minimum Size

The logo must not be reproduced smaller than **1.5 cm** in height to ensure legibility.

### 5.2 Clear Space (Safety Area)

A clear space (restricted area) must be maintained around the logo. No other graphic elements, text, or images may intrude into this zone.

### 5.3 Prohibited Uses

The logo must **not** be:
- Modified in proportions, colours, or placement  
- Cropped, fragmented, or used in parts  
- Combined with external decorative elements  
- Placed on backgrounds that compromise its contrast or legibility  

### 5.4 Versions and Backgrounds

The logo should be available in versions for **light** and **dark** backgrounds. In principle, the two‑colour logo should always be displayed on a **white background** for optimal contrast and recognition.

---

## 6. Application Examples

The brand identity is applied across a wide range of materials, including:
- Official correspondence, stationery, and memos  
- Certificates, diplomas, and awards  
- Business cards and letterheads  
- Posters, signage, and brochures  
- Electronic presentations  
- Websites and social media profiles  

Each faculty, research institute, and campus may develop its own detailed specifications within the unified framework, referencing the official *Manual de Identidad Gráfica* for correct and incorrect usage examples.
